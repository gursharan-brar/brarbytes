## 1. Gas stations / convenience stores in Canada (shift structure, prepay, ID checks, winter rushes, fuel price "shifts")

### Takeaway
The most specific and game-able Alberta detail is that **prepaying for fuel has been mandatory since June 1, 2018**, so the classic "gas-and-dash" has almost disappeared there (Calgary went from 525 fuel thefts to 2 in comparable six-month windows). The same law requires cameras and personal emergency transmitters for lone workers. Fuel prices in Alberta "shift" in two ways that players would see: the provincial 13¢/L fuel tax switches on and off every quarter based on oil prices (it was switched off on Oct 1, 2026), and retail prices cycle, with overnight jumps as large as ~35¢/L.

### Cited Findings

**Prepay law / gas-and-dash (Alberta + BC)**
- BC was the first province with a prepay law, "Grant's Law," in effect February 2008. It followed the 2005 death of attendant Grant De Patie, who was dragged about 7 km under a car while trying to stop a driver leaving without paying for **$12 of gas**. — [CBC Calgary](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/gas-and-dash-legislation-de-patie-1.4350382)
- Alberta passed Bill 19, *An Act to Protect Gas and Convenience Store Workers*, which put mandatory fuel prepayment into the Occupational Health and Safety Code. — [Blue Line](https://blueline.ca/alberta-introduces-legislation-mandating-pre-pay-on-gas-at-the-pump-4889)
- Mandatory prepay took effect across Alberta on **June 1, 2018**. Labour Minister Christina Gray said: "Fuel prepayment saves lives." — [CBC Edmonton](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/edmonton/prepaying-for-gas-now-mandatory-across-alberta-edmonton-1.4688577); [Calgary Herald via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/canada/calgary-herald/20180601/281496456966298)
- Reason given: over the previous four years, Alberta convenience-store robberies and gas-and-dash incidents had killed **five workers** and seriously injured three. — [The Safety Mag](https://www.thesafetymag.com/ca/topics/safety-and-ppe/alberta-government-says-prepaying-for-gas-protects-workers-safety/184012)
- Results: Calgary had **2** fuel-theft incidents in the first six months under the law, against **525** in the same period a year earlier. Edmonton had 6 against 483. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/5032267/gas-and-dash-alberta-pre-pay-law/amp/)
- The same legislation made **video surveillance mandatory**, plus **personal emergency transmitters for staff working alone**. — [The Safety Mag](https://www.thesafetymag.com/ca/topics/safety-and-ppe/alberta-government-says-prepaying-for-gas-protects-workers-safety/184012) (summary of coverage. The exact current OHS Code wording was not checked.)

**Tobacco / ID checks**
- Alberta Health Services guidance: staff must ask for approved ID confirming a buyer is **at least 18** before selling tobacco or vaping products. — [AHS regulation highlights PDF](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/info/ppih/if-ppih-tsvra-regulation-highlights.pdf)
- An industry-linked site says Alberta retailers must ask for photo ID from anyone who **appears under 25**. This is an advocacy source and does not match the AHS "18" framing (the AHS figure is the legal age, while under-25 is probably a carding policy). — [ACFAA page](https://acfaa.pages.dev/resources/article-current-rules-overview) (low reliability)
- Canadian "power wall" laws forced stores to cover cigarette displays. Stores where tobacco is more than half of sales can apply to become smoke shops, which are exempt if they bar minors. Maclean's says 223 stores in Alberta's sector have closed. — [Maclean's](https://macleans.ca/news/canada/dying-stores-morph-into-smoke-shops)

**Winter rushes (Calgary / Southern Alberta)**
- In one record cold snap, the Alberta Motor Association took **30,700 assistance calls** since Sunday, about **6x normal volume**. Waits for a battery boost reached **37 hours** in Calgary. — [CTV Calgary "Need a boost? That will be 37 hours"](https://calgary.ctvnews.ca/need-a-boost-that-will-be-37-hours-in-calgary-1.5720553). Another report cites **42-hour** waits for a boost, plus burst pipes. — [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/edmonton/alberta-car-battery-boost-wait-times)
- **[ANECDOTE, attributed in news]** An Okotoks gas station attendant said **25–30% more vehicles** stop on a cold day, and customers ask for windshield washer fluid, oil checks and tire-pressure checks. — [Western Wheel (Okotoks)](https://www.westernwheel.ca/local-news/deep-freeze-takes-toll-on-outside-workers-1522658) (I matched this quote to this URL from a search summary. Check it.)
- AMA guidance: **plug in your vehicle (block heater) below −15 °C** if parked outside. In extreme cold, plug in for at least 4 hours before driving. Keep the tank at least half full. — [CBC Calgary](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/alberta-motor-association-ama-warned-cold-snap-weather-freeze-1.5416728); [CBC Edmonton](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/edmonton/a-frozen-car-won-t-get-you-anywhere-here-are-some-tips-to-help-your-ride-survive-the-cold-1.6694237)
- Tire pressure drops with temperature, and Calgary tip sheets tell drivers to top up air and washer fluid before cold snaps. — [Daily Hive Calgary](https://dailyhive.com/calgary/mr-lube-travel-calgary-record-low-temperatures)

**Fuel price "shifts": tax switch and price cycles**
- Alberta runs a **quarterly fuel-tax relief program**. The province's **13¢/L fuel tax is fully suspended when WTI averages ≥ US$90**. Partial relief of **4.5¢–9¢/L** applies when WTI is $80–$89.99. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12068823/alberta-fuel-tax-suspension-2026/); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/calgary/alberta-gas-tax-relief)
- For **Q4 2026**, WTI averaged **$90.54** over the Aug 18–Sep 15 review window, so the tax was suspended from **Oct 1 to Dec 31, 2026**. The province estimated the average price would fall from **175.5¢/L to about 162.5¢/L**. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12068823/alberta-fuel-tax-suspension-2026/); [Western Standard](https://www.westernstandard.news/news/breaking-alberta-suspends-13-cent-fuel-tax-through-end-of-2026/77218). (A Daily Hive headline says "until 2027," but the body text and other sources say it ends Dec 31, 2026. — [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/calgary/alberta-suspends-fuel-tax-2027))
- In April 2026, Alberta kept its own formula while Ottawa announced a federal cut during high prices. — [Calgary Journal](https://calgaryjournal.ca/2026/04/14/alberta-sticking-to-its-gas-tax-formula-as-ottawa-announces-cut-amid-high-prices/)
- Retail gasoline in Canadian cities follows "Edgeworth" price cycles. Prices jump sharply, often **10%+ within a day or two**, then slide down slowly. In some cities the jumps land on the same weekday every week. — [IAEE / Atkinson & West exec summary](https://www.iaee.org/ej/ejexec/35-3_03%20Atkinson-West%20ExecSummary%2012-105%20Templated.pdf)
- Calgary stations were reported raising prices "not just week-to-week or day-to-day, but **hour-to-hour**" (2019). — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/5177309/gas-pump-station-prices-calgary-canada)
- Calgary prices **jumped about 35¢/L overnight** (CTV, around late April 2026). — [CTV Calgary](https://www.ctvnews.ca/calgary/article/gas-prices-in-calgary-jump-by-35-cents-per-litre-on-wednesday/)
- A GasBuddy analysis of 2019 data found **Tuesday was the cheapest day and Friday the most expensive** to buy gas in Alberta. — [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/calgary/when-buy-gas-alberta-day-week)

**Pump and customer moments (news write-ups of anecdotes)**
- **[ANECDOTE]** A Canadian customer filmed a Petro-Canada pump meter climbing while **no fuel came out**. Petro-Canada shut the pump down and refunded the customer. — [NOW Toronto](https://nowtoronto.com/news/petro-canada-customer-warns-about-faulty-gas-pump-after-being-charged-without-receiving-any-fuel/)
- **[ANECDOTE]** A Reddit poster who used to work at an Iowa convenience store described a customer who insisted the pump hadn't shut off properly and refused to pay for the last gallon. The customer paid once police were mentioned. — [Upworthy](https://amplify.upworthy.com/customer-demanded-the-company-presidents-address-after-arguing-over-one-gallon-of-gas-the-employee-gave-him-an-address-hed-regret-asking-for)
- **[ANECDOTE]** A Richmond, BC driver **pumped gas onto his bumper to wipe it down**. — [Richmond News](https://www.richmond-news.com/local-news/richmond-driver-pumps-gas-onto-bumper-to-wipe-it-down-3053245)
- **[ANECDOTE]** A Reddit-sourced story of a customer who ran out of gas and blamed the attendant. — [Bored Panda](https://boredpanda.com/rude-customer-out-of-gas-blames-cashier)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** Under Alberta's prepay law, the attendant's job is: customer prepays → cashier **authorizes pump N** → the pump stops at the prepaid amount → **the customer comes back in for change** when they prepaid more than the tank held. That loop of authorizing pumps and handing back change is a natural core action for a cashier game, and it is more authentic than the drive-off chase, which mostly no longer happens in Alberta. A pre-2018 setting, or a "the day before prepay began" (May 31, 2018) story, could make drive-offs the period detail.
- **[INFERENCE]** The **13¢ tax switching off on Oct 1** and **overnight 35¢ jumps** give a real reason for the classic "climb the ladder and change the price sign" chore. A game where the price board has to be re-lettered while customers react ("I just paid 175.5 an hour ago!") ties to "SHIFT" through price shifts.
- **[INFERENCE]** Cold-snap demand (6x roadside calls, 25–30% more traffic, washer fluid, boosts, block-heater cords, half-tank advice) suits a "winter rush" wave structure in which each customer type is a visible, solvable need.
- **[INFERENCE]** "Shift change" moments: counting the till/drop safe at handover, the lone-worker emergency transmitter, cameras. These are real workplace-safety details that could create tension without falling into "night-shift horror."

These were not researched for lack of time. The team's own gas-station worker is the best source here.

- The current wording of Alberta's OHS Code on prepay/lone workers was not checked against alberta.ca.
- No Calgary-specific day-of-week price-cycle data newer than 2019 was found.

## 2. Calgary / Alberta (chinooks, Stampede, +15, Deerfoot, wildlife, oil-camp rotations, daylight/time shifts)

### Takeaway
Calgary's most dramatic "shift" is the **chinook**: Calgary's temperature has risen **30 °C in four hours**, and nearby Pincher Creek holds the Canadian record of **+41 °C in one hour**. The most timely one is that **Alberta did not "fall back" on Nov 1, 2026**: Bill 31 made "Alberta Time" (UTC−6, permanent daylight time) law in May 2026, overriding a 2021 referendum that narrowly voted the idea down. BC also moved to permanent daylight time in 2026. Other strong local material: oil-sands camps run **14-on/7-off** rotations on days or nights, the +15 has **86 bridges and 16+ km** of skywalks, and the Deerfoot is Alberta's busiest and most crash-prone freeway.

### Cited Findings

**Chinooks**
- Canadian record: a chinook at **Pincher Creek** (about 200 km south of Calgary) raised the temperature **41 °C in one hour in 1962, from −19 °C to 22 °C**. — [Chinook wind (archived Wikipedia)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20111109212534/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinook_wind); [Reader's Digest Canada](https://www.readersdigest.ca/?p=290890)
- In Calgary on **Jan 11, 1983**, the temperature rose **30 °C (−17 °C to 13 °C) in four hours**. — [CBC Calgary "How chinook winds bring warmth"](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/calgary-chinook-valentines-day-record-high-torneby-environment-canada-1.3982463)
- A Calgary meteorologist says **20–30 °C swings are not uncommon**. Calgary averages about **25 chinook days per meteorological winter**. — [CBC Calgary](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/calgary-chinook-valentines-day-record-high-torneby-environment-canada-1.3982463)
- A distinctive cloud band, the **chinook arch**, appears in the western sky as the wind approaches, and the temperature then climbs fast. Mechanism: air rises over the western Rockies, then warms as it descends the eastern slopes. — [CBC Calgary](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3982463); [CBC interactive "Chinook"](https://www.cbc.ca/calgary/interactives/chinook)
- A weather site reported a town where the temperature jumped **20 degrees in one hour**. — [The Weather Network](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/weather/forecasts/this-town-saw-temperatures-jump-20-degrees-in-just-one-hour)
- Calgary's February record is quoted as "almost 23 degrees" (1992) by one meteorologist. Another source credits nearby Claresholm with 24 °C on Feb 27, 1992. — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3440654); [archived Wikipedia](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20111109212534/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinook_wind) (conflict noted)

**Time / daylight "shift" (very timely: three days before this research)**
- The **Oct 18, 2021** Alberta referendum on year-round daylight time **failed narrowly**: **50.2% No (536,874)** against **48.8% Yes (531,782)**, with turnout of about 38.7%. — [Elections Alberta](https://www.elections.ab.ca/senate-and-referendum/referendum-questions/daylight-saving-time/)
- In 2022 the province said it had "no plans to revisit" the question. — [CTV Calgary](https://calgary.ctvnews.ca/alberta-has-no-plans-to-do-away-with-twice-a-year-time-changes-1.5821699)
- On **April 23, 2026**, Alberta tabled legislation to end clock changes and stay on daylight time permanently, branded **"Alberta Time"** (UTC−6). Minister Dale Nally tabled it. University of Calgary circadian researchers objected and favoured permanent *standard* time, citing late winter sunrises. — [National Newswatch / CP](https://nationalnewswatch.com/2026/04/23/alberta-introduces-bill-to-ditch-clock-changes-and-stay-on-daylight-time-year-round); [infonews](https://infonews.ca/news/7623539/cp-newsalert-alberta-introduces-bill-to-stay-on-daylight-time-year-round/)
- **Bill 31 (Red Tape Reduction Statutes Amendment Act, 2026)** received **Royal Assent May 14, 2026** and was reportedly proclaimed **June 18, 2026**. Alberta **did not fall back on Nov 1, 2026** and now matches Saskatchewan year-round. The change appears in IANA tzdata 2026c (July 8). — [Yahoo News Canada](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/bill-31-alberta-moves-formalize-162621293.html); [timeanddate (via search summary)](https://www.timeanddate.com/news/time/alberta-permanent-dst.html); [Wikipedia: DST in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylight_saving_time_in_Canada). Sources disagree slightly on the proclamation milestones.
- A critical take: "Dani the decider has decided and you're getting permanent daylight savings time whether you like it or not." — [AlbertaPolitics.ca](https://albertapolitics.ca/2026/04/dani-the-decider-has-decided-and-youre-getting-permanent-daylight-savings-time-whether-you-like-it-or-not/) (opinion)
- **BC** moved to permanent daylight time as well. Its final "spring forward" was **March 8, 2026**, and clocks stayed put on **Nov 1, 2026**. BC's few mountain-time communities in the east are unaffected. — [BC Gov news release 2026AG0013](https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026AG0013-000209.htm); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/permanent-daylight-saving-time-bc); [NPR/WVTF](https://www.wvtf.org/2026-03-07/british-columbia-moves-to-permanent-daylight-saving-time)

**Calgary Stampede**
- Runs **10 days** each July, drawing about **1.4–1.48 million** visitors (1,384,632 in 2023; one source gives 1,477,953 for 2024 as a record) and about **120,000 people a day**. — [bestcalgaryhomes (fact list)](https://bestcalgaryhomes.com/calgary-stampede-number-facts) (secondary; numbers conflict by year)
- **Chuckwagon races** run each evening before the Grandstand Show, with points building over the 10 days. More than 600 chuckwagon horses are among 7,500+ animals on site. — [bestcalgaryhomes](https://bestcalgaryhomes.com/calgary-stampede-number-facts)
- **Pancake breakfasts** began **July 13, 1923**, when rancher Jack Morton drove a "Pancake Wagon" down 8th Avenue. More than **200,000 pancakes** are eaten at community events during the 10 days (2023 Stampede release). — [CTV Calgary](https://www.ctvnews.ca/calgary/article/a-tradition-that-sticks-celebrating-100-years-of-syrupy-pancake-breakfasts-at-calgary-stampede/); [UCalgary Arts](https://arts.ucalgary.ca/news/explore-origin-and-history-calgary-stampede-pancake-wagon)

**+15 skywalk**
- Calgary's **+15 network has 86 bridges and over 16 km** of elevated, climate-controlled walkways downtown (city figure). It is called the world's most extensive pedestrian skywalk system, linking about 130 buildings. Older sources give 18 km and 59–62 bridges. — [City of Calgary](https://www.calgary.ca/bike-walk-roll/plus-15-network.html?redirect=/plus15); [Wikipedia: Plus 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plus_15)

**Deerfoot Trail**
- Alberta's busiest freeway: **151,000+ vehicles/day at Memorial Drive (2025)**, about twice its design capacity (Wikipedia). The Alberta government says **180,000+ vehicles/day**. — [Wikipedia: Deerfoot Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deerfoot_Trail); [Alberta.ca](https://www.alberta.ca/deerfoot-trail.aspx)
- About **10,000 collisions from 2002 to 2007, including 24 fatalities**. Peak congestion can last up to **four hours** a day. The province planned **$478M** in upgrades. — [Wikipedia: Deerfoot Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deerfoot_Trail); [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.5057454)
- Multi-vehicle crashes regularly close the Deerfoot. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/9410028/deerfoot-trail-calgary-closed-multi-vehicle-crash)

**Wildlife on the Trans-Canada (Banff, 90 min west of Calgary)**
- Banff's 82-km section of Highway 1 has **6 wildlife overpasses and 38 underpasses** (44 crossings) plus fencing. Grizzlies, wolves, moose, elk and others have used them **150,000+ times**. **Elk adapted first. Grizzlies and wolves can take up to five years** to trust a new crossing. — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/wildlife-overpasses-underpasses-make-animals-people-safer); [Y2Y](https://y2y.net/blog/the-home-of-wildlife-crossings-banff-alberta/)
- Grizzlies, elk, moose and deer prefer **high, wide, short** crossings (overpasses). Black bears and cougars prefer **long, low, narrow** underpasses. — [discoverAPEGA](https://discoverapega.ca/stories/wildlife-crossings-key-to-highway-safety-in-banff/)

**Oil-sands camp rotations (shift work)**
- **14-on/7-off** is a common fly-in/fly-out rotation at Fort McMurray sites (Fort Hills, Kearl, Suncor). One posting lists **11.5 paid hours/day**, "days and nights." — [Energy Job Shop (Fort Hills HEO)](https://www.energyjobshop.com/job/heavy-equipment-operator-fort-hills-fly-in-camp-49-85-hr-61-hours-ot-per-rotation/); [Energy Job Shop (Kearl 14/7)](https://www.energyjobshop.com/job/kearl-site-hiring-labourers-grader-operators-more-14-7-rotation-11-5-hr-shifts-fifo-camp/)
- Other rotations include **7/7 and 14/14** (common for maintenance, pipefitters and process operators), usually on **12-hour shifts**, with flights from Edmonton or Calgary. — [Energy Job Shop guide](https://www.energyjobshop.com/articles/jobs-in-fort-mcmurray-camp-fifo-oil-sands-and-entry-level-opportunities/); [Energy Job Shop (pipefitters 14/14)](https://www.energyjobshop.com/job/pipefitters-14-14-rotation-camp-flights-fort-mcmurray-ab/); [Energy Job Shop (Calgary/Edmonton flights)](https://www.energyjobshop.com/job/labourers-camp-and-flights-from-edmonton-or-calgary-fort-mcmurray-ab/) (job-board listings, not official company policy)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** A chinook works as a mechanic: within minutes of game time, roads shift **ice → slush → water → flash-refreeze when the wind drops**, and customers change from "boost me / washer fluid / plug-in cord" to "car wash please / shorts and sunglasses." The visible chinook arch can act as a timer or telegraph. The team knows this from experience, and players elsewhere will find it surprising.
- **[INFERENCE]** **"The shift that didn't happen"**: Nov 1, 2026 was the first autumn in which neither Alberta nor BC fell back. That is fresh, local and literally about a time shift. Possible jokes: a character still running on the old time, a gas station whose night shift is suddenly an hour "off," printed shift schedules that no longer match the sun. Because both provinces moved to permanent daylight time, **[INFERENCE]** the one-hour Alberta–BC offset should still hold year-round (AB UTC−6, BC UTC−7), so crossing the Rockies still shifts the clock.
- **[INFERENCE]** The 2021 referendum that failed **50.2 to 48.8** and was then overridden by legislation adds a comedic or political layer (e.g., a "vote every shift" mechanic).
- **[INFERENCE]** The +15 suggests a vertical, "shift between levels" navigation idea (streets at −30 °C below, warm skywalks above). Stampede week suggests a "shift into cowboy mode" overnight transformation of the city, pancake rushes and chuckwagon timing.
- **[INFERENCE]** Camp rotations (14/7, days ↔ nights flip, flights home) fit a "rotation calendar" or relationship-strain story about what you miss at home while on shift.

## 3. BC mountain highways (Coquihalla, Rogers Pass, Kicking Horse, chain laws, brake checks, Highway Thru Hell, closures)

### Takeaway
BC's mountain passes come with concrete, physical, rule-driven actions: **mandatory chain-ups with signed chain-up areas** (Oct 1–Apr 30, $598 fine for skipping one), **mandatory brake checks** before the Coquihalla descent at Zopkios, a **"no trucks in the left lane"** rule on Snowshed Hill where semis spin out most, and the **Canadian Army firing 105-mm howitzers** at avalanche paths above Rogers Pass (16–18 roadside gun positions, ~270 targets). The military says it will **end that role in 2027**. The Coquihalla was **destroyed in Nov 2021** (6–7 bridges lost) and reopened to trucks in 35 days.

### Cited Findings

**Winter tire and chain rules**
- On most BC highways, winter tire and chain-carry rules run **Oct 1 to Apr 30**. Some routes outside mountain passes or high-snowfall areas end **March 31**. April-30 routes are marked with regulatory signs. — [BC Gov commercial winter driving page (via search summary)](https://gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation/driving-and-cycling/traveller-information/seasonal/winter-driving/commercial); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7338165)
- The number, type and placement of chains or traction devices depend on the vehicle's licensed GVW, type and configuration. — [BC Gov commercial page (via search summary)](https://gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation/driving-and-cycling/traveller-information/seasonal/winter-driving/commercial)
- From Oct 1, 2019, rules were clarified for **all commercial vehicles over 5,000 kg**. Fines are **$196 for not carrying chains** and **$598 for not installing chains at a mandatory chain-up**, up from a base of $121. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/5939693/b-c-truckers-face-stiffer-fines-for-lack-of-chains/); [BC Gov news release 2019TRAN0163](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2019TRAN0163-001816); [Truck News](https://www.trucknews.com/regulations/fines-increase-in-b-c-for-not-following-chain-up-regulations/1003094062) (2019 figures; 2026 amounts not confirmed)
- The BC Trucking Association described a "very large chain-up area at the bottom of Box Canyon" on the Coquihalla. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/5939693/b-c-truckers-face-stiffer-fines-for-lack-of-chains/)
- DriveBC posts live chain-up status, e.g., "Highway 5 near the Box Canyon Chain Up." — [CFJC Today](https://cfjctoday.com/?p=993588)
- "Chain up or turn back" is the official messaging to truckers. — [Truck News](https://www.trucknews.com/transportation/bc-trans-to-truckers-chain-up-or-turn-back/1003120890)
- Weight-threshold conflict: one safety guide says over 11,794 kg GVWR must carry chains, and an industry article says over 27,000 kg. The government page notes the requirements vary by vehicle. — [SafetyDriven](https://safetydriven.ca/resource/what-commercial-drivers-need-to-know-about-chains-and-winter-tires/amp/); [BCTA](https://bctrucking.com/node/3545) (conflict noted)

**Coquihalla (Highway 5)**
- **"No Trucks in the Left Lane"** began as a pilot in **Nov 2018** on **Snowshed Hill, between Box Canyon and Zopkios**, the spot where "trucks spin out the most" and where enforcement concentrates. ## 4. Trucking (hours of service / ELDs, chaining, load shifting, weigh scales, gears)

### Takeaway
Canadian federal hours-of-service rules set three clocks a driver must manage at once: **13 h driving, 14 h on-duty, and a 16 h elapsed window** per shift, plus **10 h off-duty per day** (8 consecutive) and a **70 h / 7-day cycle** that resets after 36 h off. Since **June 12, 2021**, these hours have been tracked by **electronic logging devices**, with penalties phased in through 2022–2023. Cargo law (NSC Standard 10) exists specifically to stop **loads from shifting**.

### Cited Findings
- Canada's federal limits: no driving after **13 hours of driving** or **14 hours on-duty** in a day or work shift. No driving after **16 hours have elapsed** since the start of the shift. An **8-consecutive-hour** off-duty period resets the window. At least **10 hours off-duty per day**, of which 8 must be consecutive. Up to **2 hours of off-duty time can be deferred** to the next day. — [Manitoba Gov HOS rules](https://www.gov.mb.ca/mti/mcd/carriers_drivers/hours.html); [Switchboard NSC HOS guide](https://www.onswitchboard.com/blog/canadian-hours-of-service-quick-guide); [Samsara](https://samsara.com/guides/hours-of-service-rules-in-canada)
- The 2007 federal reform cut maximum driving from **16 to 13 hours**, cut on-duty from **16 to 14**, and raised minimum off-duty from **8 to 10**. — [Truckinginfo / HDT](https://www.truckinginfo.com/news/transport-canada-publishes-new-hours-of-service); [CCJ](https://www.ccjdigital.com/business/article/14904811/canada-starts-new-hours-rules-jan-1)
- **Cycle 1: no driving after 70 on-duty hours in 7 days.** **36 consecutive hours off** resets Cycle 1 or lets the driver switch to Cycle 2. Switching back from Cycle 2 needs **72 hours off**. The governing regulation is **SOR/2005-313**. — [J.J. Keller Canadian HOS](https://jjkellercompliancenetwork.com/regsense/canadian-hours-of-service); [Manitoba HOS guide PDF](https://web40.gov.mb.ca/mti/mcd/carriers_drivers/pdf/guide/hours_service.pdf)
- By comparison, Canada's **16-hour shift window** is longer than the US **14-hour** window. — [FleetOwner](https://www.fleetowner.com/technology/article/21158575/as-canadas-eld-deadline-nears-government-plans-soft-enforcement)
- The **ELD mandate took effect June 12, 2021**, with "progressive enforcement" (education, no penalties) at first. Sources disagree on when full penalties began: **June 12, 2022** or **Jan 1, 2023** in various provinces. Maximum penalties for repeat ELD/HOS offences reach **$20,000 in Ontario** and **$25,000 for fleet carriers in Saskatchewan**. The ELD rule did **not** change the hours themselves. — [Truck News](https://www.trucknews.com/road-today/no-eld-penalties-until-june-2022-jurisdictions-confirm/); [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/no-more-delays-for-canadas-enforcement-of-eld-mandate); [Miller Thomson](https://www.millerthomson.com/en/insights/transportation-and-logistics/enforcement-of-mandatory-electronic-logging-devices-for-canadian-carriers/); [Geotab](https://www.geotab.com/blog/transport-canada-eld-mandate/)
- **Load shifting**: National Safety Code **Standard 10** sets requirements so cargo does "not **shift**, move or spill onto the roadway." Provinces adopt it by reference. Ontario's version requires at least **one tiedown per 3.04 linear metres** of lading. Canada stopped recognizing **unmarked, unrated tiedowns** from **Jan 1, 2010**. Crosswise log loads need **automatic tiedown tensioners**. Drivers must inspect cargo securement. — [Ontario Reg. 363/04](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/040363); [Truckinginfo](https://www.truckinginfo.com/news/canada-to-drop-default-wll-ratings-for-unmarked-cargo-tiedowns); [CCMTA NSC Standard 10 (2010 PDF)](https://www.ccmta.ca/web/default/files/PDF/NSC_Standard10_-_Sept_2010.pdf); [Energy Safety Canada](https://www.energysafetycanada.com/resource/cargo-securement)
- BC CVSE publishes a cargo-security driver booklet. — [CVSE Booklet 2](https://www.cvse.ca/references_publications/booklets1-4/pdf/Booklet_2_Cargo_Security.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The **three overlapping HOS clocks** (13/14/16, plus the 10-hour off-duty need and 2-hour deferral) are a ready-made resource-juggling system. A "logbook Tetris" or "beat the 16-hour clock to the next chain-up" design is authentic and goes beyond gear-shifting gimmicks.
- **[INFERENCE]** **Load shift** is the cleanest "SHIFT" pun with real safety stakes: tiedown placement (one per 3.04 m), straps that must be *rated*, and cargo sliding on a Coquihalla descent. That could be a physics loading puzzle where the challenge is packing and strapping, not driving.
- **[INFERENCE]** **Team driving / sleeper berth** offers a "shift swap" mechanic between two drivers. It suits a 3-person team that wants co-op roles.

### Gaps
- **Cycle 2 = 120 hours in 14 days** (with a required 24 h off after 70 h) is from general knowledge and was **not verified** in this session. The official Justice Laws text could not be fetched.
- Team-driving and sleeper-berth split rules, the adverse-driving-conditions extension, and the 60th-parallel-north rules were not verified.
- **Weigh scales** (e.g., BC/Alberta inspection stations, transponder bypass), **jake-brake bylaw signs**, **CB radio culture**, **dispatch**, and transmission specifics (10/13/18-speed shift patterns) were not researched in this session.

## 5. Hockey line changes (typical shift length)

### Takeaway
An NHL "shift" averages about **45–50 seconds**: **47.07 s** across 558 players in 2018–19, forwards about 46 s, defence about 48.6 s. Coaches aim for about **40 s**, power-play shifts can run **70–80 s**, and penalty killers try to stay under **30 s**.

### Cited Findings
- 2018–19 NHL.com data analysis: average shift **47.07 s** for all 558 players. Forwards about **46 s**, defencemen about **48.6 s**. — [Hockey Answered](https://hockeyanswered.com/?p=222) (blog analysis of NHL.com stats)
- Coaching ideal: about **40 s**. Mike Babcock's Leafs averaged about 40 s per forward shift. — [NHL.com / Kraken](https://www.nhl.com/kraken/news/seattle-kraken-line-changes-ft); [TSN](https://www.tsn.ca/leafs-adapting-babcock-s-40-second-shift-mentality-1.838066?tsn-amp)
- Power-play shifts can run **70–80 s**. PK shifts are kept to **≤30 s**. Players generally shouldn't change while the puck is in their defensive zone, and they change when their linemates do. — [Hockey Answered](https://hockeyanswered.com/how-do-hockey-players-know-when-to-change/); [Hockey Response](https://hockeyresponse.com/how-do-hockey-players-know-when-to-change-lines/)
- Explainer on how line changes work and why. — [Las Vegas Sun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2017/jun/03/line-changes-how-hockey-substitutions-work-and-why/)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** A roughly 45-second hockey shift is the same length as a micro-game round. A **"change on the fly"** mechanic (swap your active character before they tire, never while the puck is in your zone) could transfer to non-hockey settings, e.g., gas-station staff rotating at a till during a rush. Calgary (Flames, Saddledome, community rinks) makes hockey part of the friends' personal material.

### Gaps
- No official NHL source for league-wide average shift length was found. The figures are from blogs and media.
- No browser or jam hockey game built around line-change timing was found (see section 6).
