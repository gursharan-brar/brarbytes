## Q1. Themes, entry counts, ratings and rating categories (2023, 2024, 2025)

### Takeaway
The themes are confirmed: 2023 = SCALE, 2024 = SECRETS, 2025 = WAVES. Each year had roughly 500-710 ranked entries. All three years used the same six rating categories (Overall, Gameplay, Graphics, Audio, Innovation, Theme interpretation). A typical entry got about 14-15 ratings, and itch.io lowers the score of any game rated fewer times than the median.

**How scores are adjusted (all years)**
- itch.io results pages note that the score "is adjusted from raw score by the median number of ratings per game in the jam" — [itch.io Game Off 2024, Secondhand rating page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2024/rate/3149512)
- An itch.io forum post gives the formula: adjustment = sqrt(min(median, votes_received) / median). Any game rated fewer times than the median is marked down — [itch.io forum: Calculated ratings for jams vs raw scores?](https://itch.io/t/644401/calculated-ratings-for-jams-vs-raw-scores); [itch.io forum: How can I turn off score weighting](https://itch.io/t/2915652/how-can-i-turn-off-score-weighting-for-my-jam)
- Per-category result pages follow the pattern `/results/<category>`, e.g. `https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2025/results/gameplay?page=25` — [itch.io Game Off 2025 gameplay results](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2025/results/gameplay?page=25)

### Inferences
- Example of the penalty: 2023's #1, Glory to SCALE, had 9 ratings, a raw score of 4.778 and an adjusted score of 4.533. Plugging those into the formula (4.533/4.778 = 0.949; 0.949² = 0.90 = 9/median) gives a 2023 median of about 10 ratings per game. It still won despite the penalty. (My calculation, not stated on the page.)
- Ratings per game: 2023 averaged about 13.9 (8,765 / 630) and 2025 about 14.7. A team that rates many games and so collects 16 or more ratings avoids the penalty entirely. (Inference.)
- The other category slugs are probably `/results/innovation` and `/results/theme-interpretation`. I only saw `/results/gameplay` and `/results/overall` (the latter on another jam), so this is unverified.

### Gaps
- Exact 2024 ratings total and the official per-year medians for 2023 and 2024.
- The exact entry count for 2025 (699 vs 709).

## Q8. Game Off 2026 "SHIFT": announcement, official example ideas, dates, rules, categories, voting, past stats

### Takeaway
SHIFT was announced on November 1, 2026, for GitHub's 14th annual Game Off. Submissions are due **December 1, 2026, 13:37 PST** on itch.io, and voting ends **January 1, 2027, 13:37 PST**. The rules:
- Your game must live in a GitHub repository; open source is encouraged but not required.
- Any assets are allowed: open source, commercial, commissioned or original.
- AI-assisted development is allowed, but you must disclose it at submission.
- Solo entries and teams are both fine.
- There is the usual six-category, participant-rated voting.

I found no official requirement that the game be browser-playable.

### Inferences
- **The obvious idea is already taken.** The blog's first example, Shift-keys-only control, is the obvious reading, and at least one entrant is already building exactly that (Bit Shift). A team wanting Innovation or Theme points should twist further, the way 2024's Innovation winner invented a new input. (Inference.)
- **Shift-key controls have a Windows Sticky Keys risk.** Pressing Shift five times opens the Sticky Keys prompt on Windows. The GitHub issue above suggests entrants are already testing for this. A Phaser team using Shift as a control should test on Windows. (The Sticky Keys behaviour is general Windows knowledge, not from a cited source.)
- **Rating other games is the lever a team controls.** With a median of about 16 ratings and scores marked down below the median, ratings are a lever the team controls: rating other entries and commenting usually brings reciprocal plays. (Inference; no official "rate-to-be-rated" queue mechanic was found for Game Off.)
- **Browser builds collect more ratings.** Browser play isn't required, but almost all confirmed top entries had browser builds. Raters play more games when there is no download, which probably helps a game reach the rating median. (Inference.)
