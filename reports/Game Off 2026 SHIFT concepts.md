# Make SHIFT the verb, not the puzzle

Build a fast, single-player 2D browser game where the player performs a "shift" within five seconds of clicking Play, and where the variety comes from spawns or microgames rather than hand-made puzzle levels. My first pick is **Prepay Only**, a WarioWare-style run of five-second microgames set during one compressed shift at a Calgary gas bar. Second is **Shift Over**, a physics crowd-shoving game on a rush-hour CTrain. Third is **Boots for Posts**, a pond-hockey game where you literally shift the goalposts. Three findings drive the choice. Game Off theme winners turned the theme word into the core action: Trail of Secrets won both Overall and Theme in 2024 with one level hiding 12 secret endings, and a collection of idiom microgames won Theme in 2022. Overall winners were usually top-3 in Gameplay. And nearly every confirmed top entry since 2023 ran in the browser, where entries collect about twice as many ratings. The obvious SHIFT ideas are already crowded: world-toggling, shapeshifting, night-shift horror and Shift-key-only games appear in GitHub's own example list, in earlier jam games titled "Shift", and in at least one public 2026 repo. Your personal material is a real asset. Alberta's prepay law, the October 1 fuel-tax switch, chinooks and the Coquihalla brake checks are specific and true, and I found no existing game built on them. One engine decision has to be made before evening one: Phaser 4 has been the stable line since April 2026, and Phaser's own site tells new projects not to start on Phaser 3. You left "games we love" and "what we want people to feel" blank, so I tagged every concept with the feeling it targets. Expect the ranking to move once you fill those in.

## Winners made the theme a verb, kept it short, and ran in the browser

**Game Off themes since 2020** were MOONSHOT (2020), BUG (2021), CLICHÉ (2022), SCALE (2023), SECRETS (2024) and WAVES (2025). Entry counts have grown from about 500 to **more than 700 games in 2025**, which received **10,414 ratings, a median of 16 per game** ([GitHub Blog 2025](https://github.blog/open-source/gaming/light-waves-rising-tides-and-drifting-ships-game-off-2025-winners/); [itch.io 2025 results](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2025/results)). Expect a similar field in 2026.

**The strongest Theme results came from games where the theme word is what you do.** *Trail of Secrets* (2024) won Overall and Theme Interpretation with one HTML5 level containing "12 secret ways to end the level". It was made by two people and controlled with A/D, Space and Tab ([itch.io](https://grumelkeks.itch.io/trail-of-secrets); [results](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2024/results)). The 2022 Theme winner, *Everything! (but the kitchen sink)*, was "a smorgasbord of mini-games, all inspired by popular idioms", playable in the browser ([GitHub Blog 2022](https://github.blog/open-source/gaming/game-off-2022-winners/); [rate page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2022/rate/1806716)). The 2022 Innovation winner turned the idiom "knight in shining armour" into a mirror that reflects light beams ([rate page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2022/rate/1797048)). The 2024 Innovation winner, *Shaki Shaki Island*, invented a new input: you draw shapes on an 8×8 grid and the world reacts ([itch.io](https://rowanfuture.itch.io/shaki-shaki-island)). *Evaw* (2025, Overall #1, Theme #2) used light, sound and radio waves to move through ten levels ([itch.io](https://wafflenaut-games.itch.io/evaw)). Puns without a mechanic scored badly: *MMOonshot*, a fake MMO login screen, ranked **#241 on Theme** ([rate page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2020/rate/840206)).

**Overall rewards gameplay more than cleverness.** The Overall #1s in 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2025 ranked #1, #3, #2 and #2 in Gameplay. Their Theme ranks were #46, #78, #9 and #2 ([do;MIN(0)](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2021/rate/1299219); [Neon Square](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2022/rate/1812515); [Glory to SCALE](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2023/rate/2396670); [2025 results](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2025/results)). Theme fit multiplies your score; tight, polished play is the base it multiplies.

**The format of winners is consistent.** They are 2D or isometric, made by teams of 1–4, and short: one level (Trail of Secrets), ten levels with a 9:44 team speedrun (Evaw), or "short, atmospheric puzzles" (BEACON) ([Evaw](https://wafflenaut-games.itch.io/evaw); [GitHub Blog 2025](https://github.blog/open-source/gaming/light-waves-rising-tides-and-drifting-ships-game-off-2025-winners/)). From 2023–2025, **8 of the 9 top entries with a confirmed platform were browser-playable**. The exception, Glory to SCALE, won 2023 with only 9 ratings ([itch.io](https://liquidpixel-media.itch.io/glory-to-scale)). The most-rated top-5 game in 2020–2022 was *Catapult*, a drag-and-release arcade game with **82 ratings** that its developer called "a literal shot to the moon" ([rate page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2020/rate/836447)). Two-person teams won 2024 Overall and took #2 and #3 in 2025, so three beginners is a competitive team size. I found no Phaser games among the identified top entries; Godot and Unity dominate.

**Rater complaints are the cheapest lessons.** Raters asked that Neon Square's "first level should have no obstacles" ([rate page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2022/rate/1812515)). They flagged unclear on-screen controls in the 2022 Theme winner ([rate page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2022/rate/1806716)), a missing in-game tutorial in Arithmometer, the 2023 #2 ([comments](https://anttihaavikko.itch.io/arithmometer/comments)), and controls "too hard for casual players" in Launch Party ([rate page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2020/rate/837474)).

## Other jams show where SHIFT will be crowded, and what beats the crowd

**I found no major jam whose theme was the single word "Shift".** The closest precedents are LD35 "Shapeshift", GMTK 2023 "Roles Reversed", LD30 "Connected Worlds" and GGJ 2022 "Duality". In all of them, the most literal reading was also the most crowded. LD35 entries clustered around shapeshifting heroes and mimics ([itch.io LD35 tag](https://itch.io/games/tag-ludum-dare-35)). GMTK 2023 had several entries titled "Roles Reversed" or "You Are The Boss" ([example](https://itch.io/jam/gmtk-2023/rate/2160988); [example](https://adnan-111.itch.io/you-are-the-boss-gmtk-jam-2023)). GGJ 2022 produced *Duality*, *Polarity* and *Mirror Block* ([UAT](https://www.uattech.com/global-gam-jam-2022-productivity-through-duality)). Jam games titled "Shift" lean the same way: one in GMTK 2022 shifted "between versions of the world" ([itch.io](https://sijilum.itch.io/shift)), and one in GMTK 2025 was horror ([itch.io](https://itch.io/jam/gmtk-2025/rate/3778009)). A small university jam themed "Shift Happens" was won by a colour-shifting bullet hell ([devlog](https://struhy-xd.itch.io/rgbeater/devlog/920496/game-jam-winner-)).

**For SHIFT, the crowding has already started.** GitHub's announcement suggests Shift-keys-only games, gear-shift racing, convenience-store night-shift horror, covering a shift, and bitwise shifts ([GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/open-source/gaming/game-off-2026-the-theme-is-shift/)). A public repo already describes "Bit Shift: a 30-second two-Shift-key game" ([GitHub](https://github.com/yahoai/worrrrld-gameoff-2026)). Another entrant opened an issue to test whether Windows Sticky Keys triggers ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/mcneiljv/game-off-2026/issues/2)). The Transformation Game Jam 2026 runs this same month, so itch.io will be full of transform-themed entries ([itch.io](https://itch.io/jam/tf26)).

**The standouts shifted something players assume is fixed.** *windowframe* won LD35 Overall by shapeshifting the game window rather than a character ([itch.io](https://managore.itch.io/windowframe); [Wraithkal](https://www.wraithkal.com/the-gold-medalists-of-ludum-dare-35/)). *Pause To Play*, in the GMTK 2023 winners collection, turned the pause menu into platforms ([itch.io](https://ecremprown.itch.io/pause-to-play)). Mark Brown's favourite GMTK 2023 game, *VERSUS SNAKE*, turned the Nokia classic into first-person horror ([GMTK Substack](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam)). In *Hero: Reversed* you play the boss, and your goal is the hero's fun rather than their death ([itch.io](https://gjthegamedev.itch.io/hero-reversed)). Pause To Play's developer advises: "Don't pick your first idea, maybe not even your second" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@rhettwpilcher/pause-to-play-how-to-win-the-gmtk-game-jam-b2d53818ec82)). My reading of this evidence gives five angles for picking a concept: shift the frame rather than the content, shift whose goal you serve, shift a classic everyone already knows, shift a property rather than a position, and run two states at once instead of toggling between them. Two practical side lessons: don't title the game "Shift", because that is how the obvious entries name themselves, and plan to explain the theme reading on the title card.

## Ratings come from reach, the first minute, controls and sound

**Being played matters as much as being good.** itch.io scales each score by **sqrt(min(median, ratings) / median)** ([itch.io forum](https://itch.io/t/644401/calculated-ratings-for-jams-vs-raw-scores)). With 2025's median of 16, a game with a 4.0 raw score and only 8 ratings drops to about 2.83 (my calculation). One self-published analysis of 10 itch.io jams found that **browser entries got a median of 2.1× the ratings of download-only entries**. It also found that 78% of download-only entries were penalised by the adjustment, against 27% of browser entries ([jamshell](https://munimv2.itch.io/jamshell/devlog/1713443/browser-builds-get-about-twice-the-ratings-data-from-10-itchio-jams)). The same author found that **makers who rate many games receive about 1.85× the ratings** of makers who rate none, roughly one rating back for each rating given ([jamshell](https://munimv2.itch.io/jamshell/devlog/1713956/makers-who-rate-other-jam-games-get-more-ratings-data-from-14-itchio-jams)). Both results are correlational. In the only peer-reviewed itch.io study, covering 1,290 jams, a quality description and more developers predicted high ranking ([Vu & Bezemer, FDG 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3402942.3402981)).

**The first minute decides the rating.** Ludum Dare's rules describe the ideal game as one played "right in the browser, in a few minutes, alone" ([LD rules](https://ludumdare.com/resources/archive/rules-2017/)). Veterans say a game must be understood in under a minute ([Manasoup tips](https://itch.io/jam/manasoup/topic/1754557/10-tips-from-a-game-jam-veteran)), and one checklist asks, "Is the core loop fun in 30 seconds?" ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/game-jam-survival-guide-tools-templates)). One failed Brackeys entrant summed up the attitude of raters facing thousands of games: "Okay, quick rating, next game" ([itch.io](https://itch.io/jam/brackeys-13/topic/4626778/my-game-failed-heres-what-i-learned)). Caps Lock failed exactly this test: the fun arrived after minutes of thinking, if at all.

**Juice helps in moderation.** In a study of 3,018 players of an action RPG, both no juice and extreme juice reduced play time and enjoyment, and the medium and high levels did best ([Kao 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1875952118300879)). Phaser makes moderate juice cheap. Camera shake and flash are one call each, and particle bursts and tweens take a few lines ([Phaser cameras skill](https://unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/skills/cameras/SKILL.md); [particles skill](https://unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/skills/particles/SKILL.md)).

**Browser input has traps that matter more for a theme called SHIFT.** Pressing Shift five times opens the Windows Sticky Keys dialog, which can pull players out of the game ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/1498/disable-the-irritating-sticky-filter-keys-popup-dialogs/)). Ctrl+W closes the tab and cannot be overridden ([itch.io comments](https://keb1t.itch.io/overhealted/comments)). Arrow keys and Space scroll the itch page unless captured, which Phaser handles with `this.input.keyboard.addCapture('SPACE,UP,DOWN,LEFT,RIGHT')` ([Phaser input skill](https://unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/skills/input-keyboard-mouse-touch/SKILL.md)). Browsers block audio until the player interacts with the page, so a click-to-play screen fixes both audio and keyboard focus in one step ([Phaser audio skill](https://unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/skills/audio-and-sound/SKILL.md)). Audio is one of the six rating categories. I found no data showing that silent games score worst, but leaving out sound gives up one-sixth of the rating categories.

## Twenty-six evenings buys one verb, one arena and a juice pass

**The realistic budget is about 60–75 coder-hours.** Plan for 22–23 real evenings, since some will be lost to life. Jam guides converge on a few rules: make the core loop playable in the first third, grey-box until the mechanic is fun, freeze features before the end, and submit early ([gamedev-skills](https://www.typingmind.com/skills/gamedev-skills-game-jam); [Into Games](https://intogames.org/news/global-game-jam-key-tips)). In a survey of Global Game Jam participants, teams named a minimum viable product and scope management as their most-used practices ([Borg et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00462)). Postmortems repeat the same traps. One team built content before its core systems ([Forest foRTS](https://popoprizrak.itch.io/forest-forts/devlog/687329/post-jam-postmortem)). One developer discovered at submission time that the game had never been set up to run in the browser on itch ([Door Loop](https://gumovakachnicka.itch.io/door-loop/devlog/1028665/postmortem-what-i-learned-making-door-loop-in-96-hours)). A 2025 Game Off team found too late that members could not contribute part-time ([INOVAGames](https://www2.ufjf.br/inovagames/2026/04/07/post-mortem-game-off-2025/)).

**Pick a genre Phaser makes easy.** Phaser covers arcade-style physics with boxes and circles, tweens, particles, camera effects, unified mouse and touch input, and scaling almost for free ([Phaser arcade physics skill](https://unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/skills/physics-arcade/SKILL.md)). Tilemaps work, but the official notes list several setup gotchas: names must match the Tiled file exactly, and tilesets must be embedded ([tilemaps skill](https://unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/skills/tilemaps/SKILL.md)). Matter.js rigid-body physics is available, with its own quirks, such as positions measured at the centre of mass ([Matter skill](https://unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/skills/physics-matter/SKILL.md)). My judgement is that the low-risk genres are single-screen arcade, endless or wave-based games, microgame collections, and physics toys where the physics *is* the game. The high-risk ones are precision platformers, multiplayer, procedural generation, branching narrative, and more than about 15 hand-built levels.

**For art, consistency beats talent.** Kenney assets and ChipTone sounds are CC0, and BeepBox claims no ownership of songs made with it ([Kenney](https://kenney.itch.io/kenney-game-assets-1); [ChipTone](https://sfbgames.itch.io/chiptone); [BeepBox](https://beepbox.co/)). Past Game Off FAQs allow pre-made assets but warn that "forking a game and changing some pixels is probably not in the spirit of the jam" ([Game Off 2021 FAQ](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2021/topic/1674910/faq-frequently-asked-questions)). The 2026 rules allow AI-assisted development and ask you to disclose it when you submit ([itch.io Game Off 2026](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2026)). Art styles that hide inexperience include a strict 3–4 colour palette, thick-outline flat shapes, scanned paper drawings, and phone-recorded foley. Mixing Kenney packs with your own art usually looks worse than either alone.

### Decision for the coder before evening 1: Phaser 4 or Phaser 3

**Phaser 4.0.0 shipped on April 10, 2026. Version 4.2.1 (July 9, 2026) is the current npm release, and Phaser 3's last release was 3.90.0 in May 2025** ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/phaser)). Phaser's own comparison article says that for a new project "there's no reason to start on Phaser 3". I saw that quote only in a search excerpt ([phaser.io](https://phaser.io/news/2026/05/phaser-3-vs-phaser-4)). The public API is "mostly familiar", but several things changed underneath. FX and masks became Filters, the tint system changed, `roundPixels` now defaults to false, and `Point` became `Vector2` ([migration guide](https://unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/changelog/v4/4.0/MIGRATION-GUIDE.md)).

The trade-off matters for a coder who leans on AI tools. **Most tutorials and most AI training data are Phaser 3**, so an assistant will tend to write v3 code. To counter that, **Phaser 4 ships 28 official AI-agent skill files** in its npm package, and the README says to "point your AI coding agent at the `skills/` folder" ([README](https://unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/README.md)).

**My recommendation is Phaser 4.2.x with the official Vite + TypeScript template** (`npm create @phaserjs/game@latest`) ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@phaserjs/create-game)). Pin the version in your AI tool's rules file, and add `node_modules/phaser/skills/` to its context. If you would rather follow a specific Phaser 3 tutorial series, 3.90 still works and deploys to itch the same way. None of the concepts below depends on the choice.

## A. Seventeen concept pitches, ranked strongest first

*Everything in this section is my own design speculation, built on the evidence patterns above. Real-world details used as flavour are cited. The "closest existing games" lists come mostly from memory, and none of them were checked in this research session. Treat them as a list to check yourself (see "Research limits" below).*

Every concept passes the same filter. It has a core action you take within 5 seconds and no hand-built puzzle levels. It is single-player, in the browser, and 2D. Shift is never the only control. The order follows the weighted score in section B.

### 1. Prepay Only — *laugh / panic* — work shift + price, weather and time shifts — personal

**Pitch.** One eight-hour shift at a Calgary gas bar, squeezed into three frantic minutes of five-second microgames, each built on something that really shifts at an Alberta station.

**Core verb.** One instant action per microgame: click the right pump, drag the booster clamp, slide the new price digit. A timer bar shrinks, and the game speeds up every few rounds.

**First 30 seconds.** The player clicks a punch clock reading "6:00 AM — SHIFT START". The first instruction flashes: "AUTHORIZE!" A customer's speech bubble says "Forty on six." Eight pump buttons glow, the player clicks 6, the pump whirs, and a green "PAID" stamp lands. Four seconds later: "RE-LETTER!" The price sign reads 175.5, the new price slides in, and the player drags digits up a ladder as the wind shakes it. The real hook is that Alberta suspended its 13¢/L fuel tax on October 1, 2026, and the province estimated prices would drop from 175.5¢ to about 162.5¢ ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12068823/alberta-fuel-tax-suspension-2026/)). Next comes "BOOST!": it is −30 °C, and the player drags the red clamp to the + terminal. Then "ID?": a teen with a moustache drawn on asks for smokes, and the player slams "ID PLEASE" (Alberta's legal age is 18, per [AHS](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/info/ppih/if-ppih-tsvra-regulation-highlights.pdf)). The clock jumps to "10:00 AM", the music speeds up, and by second 30 the player has played six microgames and lost one of three lives.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Each round resolves in under five seconds. Failing is funny rather than punishing. The speed-up builds panic on purpose, and variety does the work that hand-made levels would otherwise do. The format suits raters who play for a few minutes: someone who plays 90 seconds still sees most of the game.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** The title is a punch clock, the HUD is a shift clock, and the instructions themselves are shifts: price shift, chinook shift, shift change. A 2 a.m. gag can use the fact that Alberta did not fall back on November 1, 2026, after Bill 31 made "Alberta Time" permanent ([Wikipedia: DST in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylight_saving_time_in_Canada)). Here is the new angle on the avoided "night-shift convenience store" idea: this is daytime workplace comedy built from real Alberta details, and SHIFT is stacked several meanings deep, the same way the 2022 idiom-microgame winner stacked idioms.

**Closest games and the difference.** *Everything! (but the kitchen sink)* (cited above), WarioWare, *Gas Station Simulator* (1M+ copies; [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/gas-station-simulator-rockets-to-steams-top-5-best-sellers)) and *Gas Station Story* (HTML5; [itch.io](https://issumatek.itch.io/gas-station-story)). Existing gas-station games are mostly management sims or horror, such as *Shift At Midnight* ([wiki](https://shift-at-midnight.fandom.com/wiki/Shift_At_Midnight)). None I found is comedy built on real Canadian details. Prepay has been law in Alberta since June 1, 2018, and fuel thefts in Calgary fell from 525 to 2 in comparable six-month periods ([CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/edmonton/prepaying-for-gas-now-mandatory-across-alberta-edmonton-1.4688577); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/5032267/gas-and-dash-alberta-pre-pay-law/amp/)).

**Art and audio burden.** Medium art and medium audio. You need 10–12 tiny one-screen scenes. A cheap style that works: thick black outlines, a flat four-colour palette, and drawings done on paper and scanned. Audio is one music loop played back at rising speed, a win sting, a lose sting, and your coworker recorded saying "forty on six".

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** The risk is the content treadmill. Each microgame is new code and new art, and any microgame that needs thinking (for example, date-of-birth arithmetic) turns dry. The test: build the framework (instruction word, timer bar, win/lose stamp, three lives, speed-up) and three grey-box microgames. Hand it to three outsiders for three minutes. It passes if they understand each microgame within one second of the instruction appearing and ask to go again. Also time how long microgame #3 took to build. If it took more than three hours, cap the game at eight microgames.

**Verdict.** This is the strongest combination of theme, personal material and fast fun. It also splits work well: the two non-coders can each own microgame ideas on paper from day one.

### 2. Shift Over — *laugh* — social shift — personal (Calgary)

**Pitch.** On a rush-hour CTrain, the doors stay open for eight seconds. Flick and shove polite Calgarians so everyone "shifts over" and the new passengers fit before the doors close.

**Core verb.** Drag and release a passenger to shove them. They bump the people next to them in chains.

**First 30 seconds.** The player clicks "Board". The view is top-down: a train car with benches, an aisle and two doors. A chime plays: "Please shift over to let other passengers on." Three blobs crowd the doorway. The player flicks a standing blob down the aisle, and it bumps two others, who say "sorry!" in speech bubbles. The doorway clears, the doors close with a ding, and "+3 FIT" pops up. At the next stop the passengers include a hockey bag (a large circle), a stroller (a long rectangle) and a Stampede cowboy hat (a wide body). At 25 seconds someone is stuck in the door, the doors bonk, and the car groans.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Physics chain reactions give instant cause and effect, with squishy bodies and comic voices. Every stop is an eight-second round, and the passenger mix makes the variety, not level design.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** The announcement and the "SHIFT OVER" door decal say it outright. This is a social reading of the word that isn't on the avoid list.

**Closest games and the difference.** Moving Out (furniture), packing games, and possibly Japanese train-pusher games (check these). The difference is a crowd of physics bodies plus Canadian politeness comedy. The goal is fitting people in, not delivering them.

**Art and audio burden.** Low to medium. Use geometric top-down circles with faces, and overlay hats and bags. Audio is a door chime, bump sounds, and "sorry" clips recorded by the team, which costs nothing and is reliably funny.

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** The risk is how the crowd physics feels. Matter.js crowds can jitter or jam. The test: zero-gravity Matter circles with air friction in a rectangle with two door gaps, ten circles boarding per stop, an eight-second door timer, and a count of circles stuck in the doors. Tune mass and friction for an evening. It passes if testers laugh at the chain bumps and replay without being asked. It fails if bodies vibrate or stack-lock. In that case try Arcade physics circles with simple separation.

**Verdict.** Close to the top pick. Very little content is needed, and the core verb is a joke in itself.

### 3. Boots for Posts — *laugh / tension* — idiom made literal — personal (Calgary rinks)

**Pitch.** You're the kid brother in net on a frozen Calgary pond where the goalposts are two boots. You win by secretly shifting the goalposts whenever the big kids aren't looking.

**Core verb.** Drag a boot a little, then freeze before you're caught.

**First 30 seconds.** A big kid stickhandles. His head bobs down to look at the puck, with an exaggerated ice-crunch sound, and the player drags the left boot inward. He looks up, so the player lets go. He shoots, the puck clangs off the boot ("POST!"), and he squints. On the second shot the player gets greedy and is caught mid-drag. A whistle blows, "CHEATER!" appears, the boots reset wide, and the player loses a mitten (one life).

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** It has red-light-green-light tension, the comedy of an absurdly narrow goal, and a greed loop: a smaller goal scores more points but carries more risk. Each shot is a 4–6 second round. Escalation comes from a second shooter who looks in a different direction, slapshots, and a dog that steals a boot.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** The title card reads "SHIFT THE GOALPOSTS". This is the 2022 pattern of making an idiom literally true as the mechanic. Use the "shift" variant of "move the goalposts" and spell it out on screen, because some raters will only know "move".

**Closest games and the difference.** Goalie mini-games and red-light-green-light stealth games (check these). In those you defend fairly; here, cheating is the mechanic.

**Art and audio burden.** Low. White ice, two boots, one to three kids, chunky outlines. Audio is skate scrapes, puck clacks, a whistle and a recorded "CHEATER!".

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** The risk is that it's one trick, which goes shallow after two minutes. The test: grey boxes, a shooter AI whose "looking down" window has random length, and draggable boots. It passes if players voluntarily make risky drags and laugh when they're caught. It fails if they just wait out every safe window, in which case raise the reward for greed.

**Verdict.** Strong theme reading, tiny scope. It needs a clear escalation plan to last five minutes.

### 4. Day Sleeper — *laugh / panic* — after the work shift — personal (night shifts)

**Pitch.** You just finished a twelve-hour night shift at the gas bar and it's 10 a.m. Stay asleep while the sunbeam creeps across the bed and the neighbourhood gets loud.

**Core verb.** Shift in bed (A/D, or drag) to stay out of the moving sunbeam, and click noise sources to deal with them.

**First 30 seconds.** "SHIFT ENDED 7:00 AM." A side view of a bedroom and a sleep meter. A sunbeam slides through the blinds toward the character's face; the player presses A, and the sleeper rolls left with a groan. A magpie squawks on the sill, and the player clicks to throw a slipper. The phone buzzes "can u cover tmrw??" and the player flips it face-down. That is a wink at GitHub's example, not the core of the game. A leaf blower spins up next door, and the player jams in earplugs from a limited supply. By 30 seconds the beam moves faster and two noises overlap.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Whack-a-mole chaos plus physically dodging the light. The sounds are the jokes, and a full "day" takes three minutes.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** The opening card, and shifting in bed as the verb. It is weaker than #1–3, because "shift" here reads as the setting more than the action, and expect a middling Theme score.

**Closest games and the difference.** Whack-a-mole games and WarioWare-style sleep games (check these). The difference is a believable shift-worker's morning, plus dodging the sunbeam.

**Art and audio burden.** Low art: one room and 6–8 props. Medium to high audio, because sound carries the game. That is an opening for the Audio category if someone records good foley.

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** The risks are shallowness and a weak theme reading. The test: a grey room, four noise types and a moving rectangle for the beam. It passes if testers fail and retry. Ask them afterwards what the jam theme was. If nobody says "shift", strengthen the framing.

**Verdict.** The best fallback. It has the smallest scope of any good idea here, and you can finish it even if the team loses a week.

### 5. Load Shift — *tension / laugh* — physical cargo shift — personal (Coquihalla, trucking)

**Pitch.** Get a badly strapped load down the Coquihalla to Hope without it shifting off the deck. Brake too hard and it lurches forward; brake too little and your brakes cook.

**Core verb.** Feather one brake input (hold or release) to keep a wobbling stack balanced.

**First 30 seconds.** "Leave Merritt." In side view, a semi sits fixed in the centre of the screen with hay bales, a hot tub and a canoe stacked on the flatbed as physics bodies. The road tilts down Snowshed Hill. Holding Space makes the brake-temperature gauge climb, and the stack slides forward while the canoe teeters. Letting go raises the speed and lets the stack settle. A sign appears: "BRAKE CHECK — ZOPKIOS", a real mandatory check before the descent ([SafetyDriven](https://safetydriven.ca/truck-restrictions-make-the-coq-safer/)). The player stops in the box and clicks a strap three times to ratchet it tight. Snow starts at a "CHAIN-UP AREA" sign; skipping a mandatory chain-up cost $598 under 2019 rules ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/5939693/b-c-truckers-face-stiffer-fines-for-lack-of-chains/)). At 30 seconds the canoe goes over the side.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** It's Jenga on wheels: small decisions every second, and physics comedy when things fail.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** The title is "LOAD SHIFT", with the strapline that cargo must not "shift, move or spill" (the language of the National Safety Code cargo standard; [Ontario Reg. 363/04](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/040363)). There are no gears in this game, which keeps it clearly apart from GitHub's gear-shift racing example.

**Closest games and the difference.** *Alaskan Road Truckers* already uses snow chains in a sim ([Racinggames.gg](https://racinggames.gg/news/alaskan-truck-simulator-trailer-shows-unique-survival-system/)). Old Flash-era "carry cargo over hills" truck games and Jelly Truck are close in feel (check these). The difference is the real Coquihalla rules and the one-button brake feathering.

**Art and audio burden.** Low to medium. One truck sprite, 6–10 cargo items, and 2–3 layers of parallax mountain silhouettes. Audio is an engine loop that changes pitch, air-brake hiss and cargo thuds.

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** The risk is Matter.js tuning. A cheap trick keeps it manageable: the truck stays still, the world gravity vector tilts with the road grade, and braking adds a forward force on the cargo. The test: a grey deck, five Matter boxes, a sine-wave road and one brake key. It passes if testers call it tense and replay to save the canoe. It fails if the stack is either rock-stable or collapses at random.

**Verdict.** The best pick if your target feeling is tension, and the most authentic use of the trucking material.

### 6. Howitzer Season — *tension / power* — snowpack shift — personal (Rogers Pass)

**Pitch.** It's the last winter the army keeps Rogers Pass open with artillery. Shell the snow building up above the Trans-Canada so it comes down when you choose, not when a bus is underneath.

**Core verb.** Close the highway gate, then fire on a slope.

**First 30 seconds.** Three avalanche paths sit above a side-view highway. Snow builds on path 2 with creaking and a growing cornice. The player clicks the gate (honks rise) and clicks path 2: a boom, camera shake, and avalanche particles bury the empty road. A plow clears it and the player reopens the gate. By 25 seconds, paths 1 and 3 are both loaded and a semi is crawling under 3. The facts behind it: crews fire 105-mm howitzers from roadside platforms from November to April ([DND](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/corporate/news/regional-news/western-sentinel/2023/02/caf-keeps-highways-moving-through-rogers-pass.html)), there are about 270 targets across 135 paths ([Parks Canada](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/bc/glacier/nature/controle-avalanche-control/fact)), and the military role ends in 2027 ([CP via infonews](https://infonews.ca/news/7642629/howitzers-to-fall-silent-when-avalanche-control-deal-in-rogers-pass-ends-in-2027/)).

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Missile Command-style triage with a hugely satisfying boom.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** Badly. It reads as an avalanche game. "Shifting snow" and "the gunners' last shift" are setting, not verb.

**Closest games and the difference.** Missile Command and traffic-control games (check these). The setting appears to be untouched.

**Art and audio burden.** Low to medium: silhouette mountains and particles.

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** The risk is the theme. Show five people a 10-second clip and ask what the jam theme is. If nobody says "shift", that confirms it.

**Verdict.** Fun and novel, but wrong for this theme. Steal it as a Prepay Only microgame, or as a stage in Load Shift.

### 7. Chinook Rush — *panic* — weather shift + work — personal (Calgary)

**Pitch.** Run a Calgary gas-bar forecourt while a chinook swings the temperature from −25 °C to +10 °C in minutes, and every customer's needs shift with the thermometer.

**Core verb.** Grab the right item and run it to the right pump.

**First 30 seconds.** At −25, frosted cars arrive with icons for a boost, washer fluid and a block-heater cord. The AMA advises plugging in below −15 °C ([CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/alberta-motor-association-ama-warned-cold-snap-weather-freeze-1.5416728)). The clerk runs in, grabs washer fluid and delivers it. A chinook arch forms on the horizon and the thermometer climbs. In real life Calgary went from −17 °C to 13 °C in four hours in 1983 ([CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/calgary-chinook-valentines-day-record-high-torneby-environment-canada-1.3982463)). The icons flip to car wash, slush drink and sunglasses, and slush puddles slow the clerk down. When the wind drops, everything flash-freezes and the clerk slides around.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Overcooked-style rushing, and the shift scrambles your priorities mid-run.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** Yes, through the giant thermometer, though non-Albertans need the chinook taught visually.

**Closest games and the difference.** Overcooked, Diner Dash and Gas Station Simulator (check these). I found no game that uses chinooks, but that is weak evidence, because itch.io could not be searched.

**Art and audio burden.** Medium to high: forecourt, store interior, cars, 6–8 items, weather states.

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** Scope. A service sim is only fun once the queue, carrying, patience timers and weather all exist. The test: three pumps, three items, and a thermometer that flips demand halfway. It passes if the flip produces a visible "oh no" scramble.

**Verdict.** The most original personal idea here, and too heavy on systems for 26 evenings. Use the chinook as a microgame or modifier instead.

### 8. Row Shift — *clever / panic* — spatial shift — not personal

**Pitch.** You stand on a floating 7×7 grid while slimes hop toward you. Shift whole rows and columns like a sliding puzzle to fling them off the edge, without shifting yourself into the void.

**Core verb.** Click an edge arrow to slide a row or column one tile.

**First 30 seconds.** Three slimes hop in. The player slides a row: it thunks, and a slime drops off with a splash. A combo counter appears. By 20 seconds a slime is in the player's own row, and sliding that row would carry the player too.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Crunchy slides and chain kills, and it runs in real time, so there's no stopping to solve.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** Yes: the floor visibly shifts.

**Closest games and the difference.** The Labyrinth board game and sliding-puzzle games (check these). The difference is real-time arena survival.

**Art and audio burden.** Very low, with geometric tiles and blobs.

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** It could slide back into the Caps Lock trap, with players pausing to think. Build a grey grid with one enemy type and a spawner, and measure how long players pause before each move. If the average pause is more than about a second, speed the game up or cut the idea.

**Verdict.** Good mechanics, no personality. It's the pick if you want players to feel clever. A Bow River spring-ice-breakup skin would add some local flavour.

### 9. Off By One — *panic / laugh* — keyboard / linguistic shift — not personal

**Pitch.** A typing-arcade game where your hands keep slipping one key to the right, so everything you type comes out shifted, and you have to keep up anyway.

**Core verb.** Type falling words (gas-bar orders: "WASHER FLUID") while the on-screen keyboard periodically slides its letters one key over.

**First 30 seconds.** The player types normally for 10 seconds. An alarm sounds "HANDS SHIFTED", the keyboard graphic animates, and now pressing F types G. Typos explode in comic gibberish.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Stroop-test panic and laughing at your own mistakes. Some players will find it frustrating instead.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** Yes, and the angle is new: the whole keyboard shifts, and the Shift key is never used.

**Closest games and the difference.** ZType and The Typing of the Dead (check these). GitHub mentioned typing games among the 2025 top ten ([GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/open-source/gaming/light-waves-rising-tides-and-drifting-ships-game-off-2025-winners/)).

**Art and audio burden.** Very low.

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** It may frustrate, it is unplayable on mobile, and keyboard focus inside the itch iframe needs care. Grey-box it in one evening. It passes if testers laugh rather than quit.

**Verdict.** Cheap and on theme, but polarizing.

### 10. V-Hold — *panic* — frame shift — weak personal

**Pitch.** Play Breakout on Grandpa's basement TV, whose picture keeps rolling and sliding sideways. Keep the ball alive with one hand and twist the V-HOLD knob with the other.

**Core verb.** Move the paddle with the mouse and correct the drift with the wheel or Q/E.

**First 30 seconds.** Normal Breakout, then a hum as the picture starts sliding left. The player's paddle aim is now off, and they twist the knob back.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Juggling two inputs, and a classic everyone already knows (the VERSUS SNAKE formula).

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** Partly. It may read as "glitch", which was really 2021's BUG theme.

**Closest games and the difference.** Glitch-aesthetic games (check these).

**Art and audio burden.** Very low. Phaser 4 Filters add blur and glow in about two lines ([filters skill](https://unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/skills/filters-and-postfx/SKILL.md)).

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** Two-handed frustration. Grey-box Breakout with drift and see whether players can manage both inputs.

**Verdict.** A respectable frame-shift idea with a muddy theme reading.

### 11. Change the Subject — *laugh / cringe* — social / cultural shift — personal (Alberta)

**Pitch.** Christmas dinner with the Alberta relatives. Shift the conversation to safe topics before it hits the topics that start fights.

**Core verb.** Drag a safe-topic card ("How about this chinook, eh?") onto a relative whose speech bubble is drifting red.

**First 30 seconds.** Uncle's bubble shows a clock icon: the 2021 daylight-time referendum, which "No" won 50.2% to 48.8% ([Elections Alberta](https://www.elections.ab.ca/senate-and-referendum/referendum-questions/daylight-saving-time/)) before the province legislated permanent time anyway. The player drags "weather" onto him just in time, while Aunt's bubble shifts to gas prices.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Plate-spinning plus recognisable family comedy.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** Weakly. The title has to carry it.

**Closest games and the difference.** Social and dinner-party sims (check these).

**Art and audio burden.** Medium: five relatives with expressions.

**Biggest risk and the 2-evening test.** Reliance on text, and humour that may not travel outside Alberta. Play it as a paper prototype with cards at a real table, then grey-box it with icons only.

**Verdict.** Charming, but text-dependent and slow to land.

### 12. Price War — *clever (dry)* — economic shift — personal

**Pitch.** Two gas stations face each other across an intersection. Set your sign price as the rival jumps 35¢ overnight, and watch cars stream to whoever is cheaper.

**Core verb.** Click the price up or down.

**First 30 seconds.** Numbers move and cars choose a station. Real retail prices do jump sharply and then slide ([IAEE](https://www.iaee.org/ej/ejexec/35-3_03%20Atkinson-West%20ExecSummary%2012-105%20Templated.pdf)), and Calgary prices once rose about 35¢/L overnight ([CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/calgary/article/gas-prices-in-calgary-jump-by-35-cents-per-litre-on-wednesday/)).

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** It mostly isn't: it's thinking, not doing.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** Moderately.

**Closest games and the difference.** Tycoon pricing games (check these).

**Art and audio burden.** Low.

**Biggest risk.** It is Caps Lock again: clever on paper, dry to play. **Verdict: weak.** Keep the physical price-sign re-lettering as a microgame instead.

### 13. Red Shift — *clever* — science shift — not personal

**Pitch.** A space shooter where your gun only damages enemies moving toward you (blue-shifted), so you steer to make them approach.

**Core verb.** Steer and shoot.

**First 30 seconds.** Red enemies shrug off your shots. A blue one charges you and pops.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** The shooting is fine; the rule underneath it is a physics lecture.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** As a colour mechanic, and colour shift is a crowded lane ([RGBeater](https://struhy-xd.itch.io/rgbeater/devlog/920496/game-jam-winner-)).

**Closest games and the difference.** Ikaruga-style polarity shooters (check these).

**Art and audio burden.** Very low.

**Biggest risk.** Raters won't get the Doppler idea. **Verdict: weak to middling.**

### 14. Change on the Fly — *tension* — hockey shift — personal

**Pitch.** Arcade 2-on-2 hockey where your skater visibly tires over a roughly 45-second shift, and you must change on the fly before you turn to sludge, but never while the puck is in your zone.

**Core verb.** Skate, pass, and dash to the bench.

**First 30 seconds.** By 35 seconds the player's skater is slowing and their shots are going wide. The player races to the bench, a fresh skater jumps out, and play continues. NHL shifts average about 47 seconds, and coaches want about 40 ([Hockey Answered](https://hockeyanswered.com/?p=222); [TSN](https://www.tsn.ca/leafs-adapting-babcock-s-40-second-shift-mentality-1.838066?tsn-amp)).

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Hockey is fun, but only once the opponent AI and puck physics are good.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** Only to hockey fans.

**Closest games and the difference.** Hockey Manager 20|20 already offers real-time line changes ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1014050/_/); title match from a search summary, check it).

**Art and audio burden.** Medium.

**Biggest risk.** The AI and physics scope. **Verdict: weak for this team.** Use the 45-second shift as a timer gag somewhere else.

### 15. Logbook — *clever (dry)* — time shift — personal (trucking)

**Pitch.** Drive Calgary to Vancouver while juggling Canada's three hours-of-service clocks: 13 hours driving, 14 on duty, and a 16-hour window, plus 10 hours off ([Manitoba HOS](https://www.gov.mb.ca/mti/mcd/carriers_drivers/hours.html)).

**Core verb.** Choose when to drive, rest and refuel.

**First 30 seconds.** Reading three meters.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** It isn't. It's a spreadsheet.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** As a time-and-shift game, yes.

**Closest games and the difference.** Truck sims (check these).

**Art and audio burden.** Low.

**Biggest risk.** It repeats every Caps Lock failure mode. **Verdict: weak.** Including it here is a warning, not a pitch.

### 16. Rotation — *melancholy* — emotional shift — personal (oil camps)

**Pitch.** Fourteen days at camp and seven at home, compressed. Each time you fly home, your family has shifted a little. Fourteen-on, seven-off is a common Fort McMurray rotation ([Energy Job Shop](https://www.energyjobshop.com/articles/jobs-in-fort-mcmurray-camp-fifo-oil-sands-and-entry-level-opportunities/)).

**Core verb.** Small work and home vignettes.

**First 30 seconds.** Mostly reading.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** It isn't fun moment to moment. The payoff comes after several minutes, which is exactly the problem.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** As a rotation story, but slowly.

**Closest games and the difference.** Narrative vignette games (check these).

**Art and audio burden.** Medium to high, with lots of scenes and writing.

**Biggest risk.** Raters quit before the emotional payoff. **Verdict: weak for a jam.** It might be a good post-jam project.

### 17. Vibe Shift — *flow* — cultural / music shift — not personal

**Pitch.** DJ a basement party by crossfading genres to keep the dancers' moods up.

**Core verb.** Crossfade between tracks.

**First 30 seconds.** Dancers change colour as the player moves the fader.

**Why it's fun moment to moment.** Only if the music is great.

**Reads as SHIFT in 10 seconds.** Loosely.

**Closest games and the difference.** Plenty of DJ and rhythm games exist (check these).

**Art and audio burden.** Very high audio: several loopable tracks from a beginner composer.

**Biggest risk.** Audio scope. **Verdict: weak.**

## C. Top three, and the smallest test that proves each is fun

**1. Prepay Only** ranks first because it is the only concept that scores at the top on both of the things the evidence says decide jam ratings. It hands you a new action every five seconds, so a rater who plays for 90 seconds sees most of the game. Its theme reading follows the pattern of the 2022 Theme winner, a stack of short games built on one word. It also uses your most authentic material: prepay pump authorization, the October 1 tax switch and price-sign re-lettering, boosts at −30, carding. **Find-the-fun test (evenings 1–2):** build a microgame runner that shows an instruction word, gives a 5-second timer, stamps WIN or LOSE, tracks three lives, and speeds up 10% every three wins. Add three grey-box microgames: AUTHORIZE (click the pump number that was said), BOOST (drag a red circle onto a + circle), and RE-LETTER (drag one digit tile into a slot). Use no art, just coloured rectangles and one jsfxr beep each. Three outsiders play for three minutes. It's fun if at least two of them say "again" without being asked and nobody needs an explanation. While the coder builds this, the two non-coders write 15 microgame ideas on index cards and act them out with a phone timer. Cut any card that takes more than a second to understand.

**2. Shift Over** ranks second because its core action is the joke itself, its content is just a list of passenger types, and its physics comedy suits raters who want instant cause and effect. The risk is concentrated in one place, crowd feel, and a grey box can answer it fast. **Find-the-fun test (evenings 3–4):** a Matter world with zero gravity and air friction around 0.1, a rectangle "car" with two door gaps, ten circles spawning at the doors each stop, drag-and-release impulses on click, and an eight-second door timer that counts circles overlapping the door line. It's fun if testers laugh at chain bumps within the first minute and play three or more stops without being asked.

**3. Boots for Posts** ranks third because it makes an idiom literally true, the pattern behind 2022's Innovation and Theme winners. It needs only one screen and a handful of sprites, and the sneak-and-freeze action produces both tension and laughs. **Find-the-fun test (one evening):** two draggable rectangles as boots, a shooter rectangle whose "head" flips down for a random 0.8–2.0 seconds, a puck fired at a random point between the boots, and a "CHEATER" state if a boot moves while the head is up. It's fun if players start taking greedy risks on their own. If they just wait for the safe window every time, double the points for a narrow goal and test again.

**How to run the decision.** Test #1 and #2 in code over evenings 1–4, play both as a team on evening 5, and lock one by evening 7. That matches the "core loop by the first third" rule. Test Boots for Posts only if both of the others fall flat. Keep **Day Sleeper** as the emergency fallback if the team loses a week, and **Load Shift** as the swap-in if your answer to "what should people feel" turns out to be *tension* rather than *laughter*. If the answer is *clever*, Row Shift is the only strong option, and it gives up all your personal material. If the answer is *cozy* or *moving*, nothing here is strong. The evidence says slow, reflective feelings struggle with raters who play for a few minutes. When you fill in "games we love", favour Shift Over or Load Shift if you love physics party games, and Prepay Only if you love fast party collections.

## Conclusion

Caps Lock didn't fail because puzzles are bad; Puzzle is among the genres that rank well on itch.io ([Vu & Bezemer](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3402942.3402981)). It failed because the theme lived in an idea the player had to work out, rather than in an action the player does every second. The pattern that wins Game Off's Theme category points the other way. Your strongest assets are not engine skills but things nobody else entering has: knowing what "forty on six" sounds like, why the price sign changed on October 1, and what Zopkios means to a trucker. Two of the top three concepts are built on that material, and the third (Shift Over) still uses Calgary.

The practical takeaway is to treat the next four evenings as an experiment, not as production. One coder with AI help can grey-box two concepts by evening 4. Meanwhile the artists can test microgame cards with a phone timer and settle on one palette, so that by evening 7 the team is building something it has already watched outsiders laugh at. After that, success depends less on the concept than on discipline. Upload a browser build in week 1, freeze features around evening 18, and have all three of you rate other entries from the first day of voting.
